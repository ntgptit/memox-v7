Elevation

Four levels, and they are dp, not shadows. A shadow is what a level renders as, and it renders differently in each mode — keeping the scale separate from the paint is what lets dark opt out of shadows without opting out of the scale.

This token was asked for by the checklist and never built, and its absence was read as a rule for two milestones: two doc comments said the surface ladder worked "without a shadow being asked to carry the hierarchy", and both were cited as a ban. It never was one. The ladder can carry the hierarchy; that is not the same as a rule against shadows.

Light
--mx-shadow-none 0 dp · Flush with the surface behind it. The default for everything.
--mx-shadow-card 1 dp · A card in a list. The lowest step that reads as a step at all.
--mx-shadow-raised 3 dp · Deliberately lifted above its neighbours — selected, dragged.
--mx-shadow-overlay 8 dp · A sheet or dialog over the whole screen.
Dark
--mx-shadow-none 0 dp · Flush with the surface behind it. The default for everything.

alpha = 0.06 + 0.01 × level, blur = 3 × level, offset-y = level. One shadow per level, not Material's two: the second is an ambient wash that costs a full-size blur per surface and, at level 1, moves the result by under half an L* step — measurable, not visible, and a list of twenty cards pays for it twenty times.

Why dark paints nothing

Measured, not assumed. The dark page sits at L* 3.9 — the bottom of the scale — so there is no room below it for a shadow to occupy. At alpha 0.10 a dark shadow moves the page by ΔL* 0.26; at 0.70, still only 2.04. The surface step already there is ΔL* 7.70. A dark shadow is paint nobody can see, and Material 3 drops it for the same reason.

Light is the opposite, and the alpha is solved for rather than picked. Light's surface step is only 2.15 once the card carries a seed tint, so the shadow has to make up the remaining ~5.6 — alpha 0.07, giving 7.75 in total. It has been re-solved twice: 0.12 overshot to 13.28, and 0.05 was right only while the card was pure white.

So the modes stay symmetric in the thing that matters. They are no longer matched on border contrast — light's border is now the lighter of the two — but on how far a card is lifted off the page in total, which is what a reader actually perceives.
