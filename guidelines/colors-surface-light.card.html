Surfaces — light

Four tiers, and the page is the only one allowed a strong navy saturation. Every surface above it drops to 20–30% saturation while climbing in lightness, so the navy reads as the room the content sits in rather than as a tint applied to the content itself. A palette where card, tile and input are all as navy as the page has no hierarchy left to spend — everything is equally coloured, so nothing is emphasised.

Light inverts the ordering: the card is the brightest thing and the rest sit below it. That is the same ordering of prominence as dark, built the only way white allows — and it is why the ladder's steps are small here and the shadow does the rest of the work.

Every role is declared. ColorScheme.fromSeed had been generating a neutral-grey container ladder with no relation to the navy canvas, and none of it was visible until the app grew a dialog, a sheet, a navigation bar and a chip — by which point it would have been expensive to discover.

The card is not pure white, and has not been since M4.10i. #FFFFFF carries no hue at all, so the one surface the whole app is built on had no relation to the seed while every other neutral did — the audit's largest finding, and the reason light mode read as a different palette from dark. #FBFBFE is seed @ 0.02 over white: hue 240, chroma 0.012.

It costs lightness. A tinted card is a darker card, so the surface step drops from 3.46 L* to 2.15. That was the argument for leaving it alone while the step was the only depth cue light had. It is not any more: the shadow's alpha was re-solved to 0.07 and the total lift is 7.75 L* against dark's 7.70.
