Surfaces — dark

Four tiers, and the page is the only one allowed a strong navy saturation. Every surface above it drops to 20–30% saturation while climbing in lightness, so the navy reads as the room the content sits in rather than as a tint applied to the content itself. A palette where card, tile and input are all as navy as the page has no hierarchy left to spend — everything is equally coloured, so nothing is emphasised.

Dark climbs L* 3.9 → 11.6 → 19.0 → 26.3, roughly 7.4 per step, so a card reads as a card and an inset tile as an inset on the strength of the ladder alone.

Every role is declared. ColorScheme.fromSeed had been generating a neutral-grey container ladder with no relation to the navy canvas, and none of it was visible until the app grew a dialog, a sheet, a navigation bar and a chip — by which point it would have been expensive to discover.

Why the ladder is measured in L*, not in contrast ratio. A deep navy page sits at luminance 0.004, and down there WCAG's +0.05 constant compresses every real step into "1.1-something": the card is 3× the page's luminance and still scores 1.17:1. L* is the perceptual scale and stays honest at the bottom, which is why the theme test asserts the ladder in L*.

Dark paints no shadow at any elevation. Measured, not chosen: at the bottom of the lightness scale a shadow moves the page by ΔL* 0.26, where the surface step already moves it 7.70. Dark keeps its ladder and a stronger border; light gives some border back and gains a shadow.
