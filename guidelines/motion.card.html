Motion

Short and few. During a review session the user is answering, not watching; animation that draws attention to itself costs recall. Three durations, and 320ms is the ceiling — there is nothing in this app worth a third of a second of somebody's attention.

Motion is also never the only carrier of a state. A spinner says "working", and so does the label beside it; a progress bar moves, and the "7 of 20" beside it says the same thing in words.

Durations
--mx-duration-fast 120ms
State changes that must not feel like a jump — a press, a hover, a colour shift on focus.
--mx-duration-normal 200ms
Card and surface transitions. A progress bar filling. A dialog arriving.
--mx-duration-slow 320ms
The longest anything in the app is allowed to take.
Easing
--mx-ease-standard Anything that starts and ends on screen. The default.
--mx-ease-decelerate Something arriving — a sheet, a dialog, a row appearing.
--mx-ease-accelerate Something leaving. Eases in, then goes.

These name the curve the platform was already using rather than introducing a new one: Flutter's implicit animations default to Curves.easeInOut, and the app never overrode it. Stating them is what stops a component reaching for the browser default ease, which is faster out of the gate and makes a 120ms press look like a flicker.

Reduced motion
  • Durations collapse to 1ms, not 0. A zero-length transition never fires transitionend, and a component that sequences off that event stops sequencing for exactly the users who asked for less motion.
  • The spinner slows; it does not stop. A stopped spinner is a lie about whether the app is doing anything, and it is the only signal the user has.
  • An indeterminate progress bar pulses in place. A bar that stops moving says "finished", which is the one thing it must not say.
  • Everything else simply arrives at its end state. Nothing in the app depends on the journey.
